Background & Research

The Schild Name

Before we can trace this specific family’s line, it helps to know where the name itself comes from, its earliest records, and who else has carried it.

This section is general surname history, not confirmed family lineage. Everything below describes the Schild surname broadly — its meaning, early records, and famous bearers — drawn from public genealogical, historical, and biographical sources. None of the people or records here are confirmed to be part of this specific family’s line; treat it as background and research starting points, the same way the crest above is a decorative motif rather than a documented coat of arms.

What “Schild” Means

Schild is German (and Swiss German / Dutch) for “shield,” from Middle High German schilt. As a surname it most likely arose one of two ways: as an occupational name for someone who made or painted shields, or, more commonly, from the old European custom of identifying a house by a carved or painted sign rather than a street number. A family living at “the house with the shield” sign would come to be known by it over generations. Among Ashkenazi Jewish families in German-speaking lands, who were required to adopt fixed surnames in the late 1700s–1800s, Schild was one of many sign- and object-based names adopted at that time. Related surnames include Schilder (shield maker/painter), Schildt, Schildknecht, and the English Shield.

Surname Through Time

A short chronology built only from verified, publicly documented references to the surname — not this family’s own record.

  1. 1294 Johannes dem Schilt is recorded as a Burger (citizen) of Hannover, Germany — the earliest documented instance of the surname in general genealogical references, nearly 730 years ago.
  2. 1482 A later record shows a Casper Schilde in Wiederitzsch, Germany. Spelling was not standardized in this era, so early records appear under many forms — Schilt, Schilde, Schildt, Schild — often for the same family, as clerks wrote names phonetically.
  3. c. 1600 Swiss records around Neuchâtel begin, part of the name’s long presence in Switzerland alongside Germany.
  4. Late 1700s–1800s Ashkenazi Jewish families in German-speaking lands are required to adopt fixed surnames; Schild is one of many sign- and object-based names adopted at this time.
  5. 1800s–early 1900s A wave of German-speaking emigration carries the name to the United States and elsewhere, alongside continued growth in Germany, Switzerland, Austria, and the Netherlands.
  6. Today Roughly 14,000–22,000 people carry the Schild surname worldwide (estimates vary by source); about 8 in 10 still live in Europe, concentrated in Germany — especially North Rhine-Westphalia, Hesse, and Lower Saxony.

A confirmed line back to any of these early records would take real archival research — parish registers, city citizenship rolls, emigration records — rather than public surname databases.

Notable Schilds Through History

People named Schild have shown up in a range of fields. None are known to be relatives of this family; they’re listed here simply because they share the name. Photos are only shown where a freely-licensed one exists (public domain or Creative Commons on Wikimedia Commons); credits are noted below each photo.

  1. AS

    Alfred Schild 1921–1977

    Austrian-American physicist at the University of Texas; co-discovered the Kerr–Schild spacetime metric and introduced “Schild’s ladder,” a construction in differential geometry still used today (and the namesake of a Greg Egan science-fiction novel).

  2. ES

    Erwin Schild 1920–2024

    German-born Canadian Conservative rabbi and author, among the last Kindertransport survivors to lead a congregation.

  3. RS

    Rolf Schild 1924–2003 OBE

    German-born British businessman, a Jewish refugee from Nazi Germany who founded Huntleigh Technology, a medical equipment company based in Luton, England.

  4. RS

    Romuald Schild 1936–2021

    Polish archaeologist and professor at the Institute of Archaeology and Ethnology, Polish Academy of Sciences, known for prehistoric Sahara and Nubian research.

  5. TS

    Thekla Schild 1890–1991

    German architect; in 1913 became the second woman in Germany to earn a degree in architecture. (Wikipedia has a photo of her, but it’s used there under fair-use terms that don’t allow reuse elsewhere, so it isn’t shown here.)

  6. Marlies Schild b. 1981

    Austrian World Cup alpine ski racer (slalom & giant slalom specialist); four Olympic medals and five World Cup season titles. Now competes under her married name, Marlies Raich.

    Photo: Special Olympics 2017, via Wikimedia Commons (CC0 / public domain).

  7. Bernadette Schild b. 1990

    Austrian World Cup alpine ski racer.

    Photo: Christian Jansky, via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0).

  8. Martina Schild b. 1981

    Swiss World Cup alpine ski racer, competing in downhill and super-G.

    Photo: GraceKelly, via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0).

  9. JS

    Jerry Schild 1954–2012

    American NASCAR Cup Series driver from Houston, Texas.

Sources: Geneanet · Forebears · SurnameDB · Wikipedia: Schild · Wikipedia: Alfred Schild
