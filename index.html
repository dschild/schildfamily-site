A Note on the Shield & Crest

The shield design used throughout this site (an azure, or blue, field with a red horizontal band and three small silver escutcheons) is inspired by a heraldic blazon historically associated with the Schild / Schildt name. The fuller crest artwork below (with the wolf and lion, raven, and “Faith · Family · Freedom” motto) is original artwork made for this site. Both are used decoratively, as a nod to the name’s meaning. Neither has been verified as a grant of arms belonging to this specific Schild family line, and both should be understood as thematic motifs rather than a documented family coat of arms.

The Schild Name History & Facts

Before we can trace this specific family’s line, it helps to know where the name itself comes from, its earliest records, and who else has carried it.

General Surname History, Not Confirmed Family Lineage

Everything in this section describes the Schild surname broadly: its meaning, early records, and famous bearers, drawn from public genealogical, historical, and biographical sources. None of the people or records below are confirmed to be part of this specific family’s line. Treat it as background and research starting points, the same way the shield and crest above are decorative rather than documented.

What “Schild” Means

Schild is German (and Swiss German / Dutch) for “shield,” from Middle High German schilt. As a surname it most likely arose one of two ways: as an occupational name for someone who made or painted shields, or, more commonly, from the old European custom of identifying a house by a carved or painted sign rather than a street number. A family living at “the house with the shield” sign would come to be known by it over generations. Among Ashkenazi Jewish families in German-speaking lands, who were required to adopt fixed surnames in the late 1700s–1800s, Schild was one of many sign- and object-based names adopted at that time. Related surnames include Schilder (shield maker/painter), Schildt, Schildknecht, and the English Shield.

Where the Name Is Found Today

Roughly 14,000–22,000 people carry the Schild surname today (estimates vary by source), and about 8 in 10 still live in Europe. Germany holds the largest concentration by far, especially North Rhine-Westphalia, Hesse, and Lower Saxony, followed by Switzerland (with records around Neuchâtel dating back to roughly 1600), Austria, and the Netherlands. A few thousand more live in the United States today, consistent with the broader wave of German-speaking emigration in the 1800s and early 1900s.

Earliest Known Records

The earliest documented instance of the surname found in general genealogical references is Johannes dem Schilt, recorded as a Burger (citizen) of Hannover, Germany, in 1294, nearly 730 years ago. A later record shows a Casper Schilde in Wiederitzsch, Germany, in 1482. Spelling was not standardized in this era, so early records appear under many forms, including Schilt, Schilde, Schildt, and Schild, often for the same family as clerks wrote names phonetically. These are general historical records of the surname anywhere it appears, not records belonging to this specific family; a confirmed line back to any of them would take real archival research (parish registers, city citizenship rolls, emigration records) rather than public surname databases.

Notable Schilds Through History

People named Schild have shown up in a range of fields. None are known to be relatives of this family; they’re listed here simply because they share the name. Photos are only shown where a freely-licensed one exists (public domain or Creative Commons on Wikimedia Commons); credits are noted below each photo.

AS

Alfred Schild (1921–1977)

Austrian-American physicist at the University of Texas; co-discovered the Kerr–Schild spacetime metric and introduced “Schild’s ladder,” a construction in differential geometry still used today (and the namesake of a Greg Egan science-fiction novel).

ES

Erwin Schild (1920–2024)

German-born Canadian Conservative rabbi and author, among the last Kindertransport survivors to lead a congregation.

RS

Rolf Schild (1924–2003) OBE

German-born British businessman, a Jewish refugee from Nazi Germany who founded Huntleigh Technology, a medical equipment company based in Luton, England.

RS

Romuald Schild (1936–2021)

Polish archaeologist and professor at the Institute of Archaeology and Ethnology, Polish Academy of Sciences, known for prehistoric Sahara and Nubian research.

TS

Thekla Schild (1890–1991)

German architect; in 1913 became the second woman in Germany to earn a degree in architecture. (Wikipedia has a photo of her, but it’s used there under fair-use terms that don’t allow reuse elsewhere, so it isn’t shown here.)

Marlies Schild (b. 1981)

Austrian World Cup alpine ski racer (slalom & giant slalom specialist); four Olympic medals and five World Cup season titles. Now competes under her married name, Marlies Raich.
